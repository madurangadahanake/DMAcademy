🕉️ Indian Music — Hindu & Mohamed Period

Hindus believed that music had been created by Brahma. So they regarded music as a divine legacy. Even Hindu Kings and Emperors studied music, and some became proficient musicians. Such a king was Samudraguptha (330–375 A.D.), a prominent Veena player. The writer of Amarakosh, Pt. Amara Singhe, and the poet Kalidasa were in the service of King Vickramadithya Samudra Guptha's Court (375–413 A.D.). There were professional musicians in king's places who received great favours from kings.

Before the invasion of Muslims during the 9th and 10th centuries, Bharathiya Sangeeth had risen to a grand status. Indian Music was influenced by Jayadeva's "Geetha Govinda". The composers of Bhakthi Geeth also rendered great service for the progress of Indian music, such as Vidyapati (1368–1450), Kabir (1440–1586), Chandidas (1417–1477), Surdas (1478–1586), Chaitanya (1486–1534), Tulsidas (1573–1623), Tukaram (1609–1649), Meerabai (1560–1630), etc. These glorious names glitter in the history of Indian music forever.

Bharathiya sangeeth survived even in the eras of invaders. Yet most Mughal kings did great favours for the progress of music, such as Allahuddin Kilji (1296–1316). The great musician Amir Khusro was in his service. Khusro's work in the music field is unique. He was a musician of genius, versatile. He modified and modernized Hindustani music by mixing the taste of Persian and Arabic music. However, there were a few rulers who ignored music and musicians. Emperor Ourangasheb not only drove away musicians from his palace but also prohibited music throughout his empire. He was recognized as the "Assassin of Music".

The golden era of Hindustani music was seen during the reign of the great Akbar (1556–1605), the greatest of all musicians — Thansen was in his service. All the ministers of Akbar's court were musicians. Akbar himself was a prominent musician and "Nakkara" player. Other great singers who lived during his reign were Nayak Baiju, Ramdas, Bashbahadur, Tan Tarang Khan, Meerabai, and Haridas Swami, who lived in Brindavan and was a great teacher of music (Nayak).

🎶 Systems of Indian Music Hindustani & Karnataki

Till the 13th century A.D. there was only one system of music prevalent all over India, known as Bharathiya Sangeeth Paddathi. Accordingly, Grama-Moorchana-Jathi system had been functioning in North and South. After the time of Sharanga Deva (13th century), in the South, one Ramamatya introduced the Thata-Raga system (Janaka Mela and Janya Raga) in place of the Grama-Moorchana-Jathi system. In the North, one Narada (author of Sangeeth Makaranda) introduced the Raga-Ragini system of music. The two systems were known as Hindustani and Karnataka.

Ahobala, Hatidaya Narayana introduced the Thata-Raga system of the South in the North, but it was in vain. The Raga-Ragini system was prevalent till the 19th century. In 1813, Mohammed Raja, a nobleman of Patna, exploded a new Raga-Ragini system of music.

In the 20th century, Chatur Pandit V.N. Bhatkhande realized that the Raga-Ragini system was not scientific, and he boldly introduced the Thata-Raga system in the North from 72 Thatas of Vyankatamukhi, selecting only 10 for the classification of all ragas. North Indian music is based on the Thata-Raga system and is in vogue today.

🔍 Hindustani vs Karnataki — Similarities & Differences

Both systems of music have been in vogue since the time of Sharangadeva.

✅ Similarities

  • Both systems recognize 12 notes (Shuddha and Vikrit).
  • Both systems construct Thatas from 12 Notes.
  • Grama, Moorchana, Jati system is no longer current in both systems.
  • Both systems obtain Ragas according to the principle of Thata-Raga.
  • Both systems recognize nine Jatis of Raga and obtain mathematically 34,848 Ragas in all.
  • Although the names of the ragas are different, the nature remains similar.
  • Akar and Nom Tom Alaps employ in both systems.
  • This is the way as to how the Ragas are sung (Swaravistar / Song / Tans & Alaps).
  • Dhrupad of Hindustani Music and Kirtanam of Karnataki Music originated from the ancient Prabandha Gayan.
  • In both systems, some forms of classical are considered noble and chaste (Dhrupad in North, Kirthanam in South).
  • In both systems, classical music as well as light music are in vogue.
  • Vadi-Swara and characterize the musical phrases are used in both systems for the recognition of Ragas.
  • The renowned poet Jayadeva's Ashta Padas are sung in both systems.

📖 Important Musical Terms

🎯 Vadi

Vadi is the predominant and the life note of the raga. It is most frequently used and long stoppages are done on it. Vadi note discharges five functions. It determines the name of the raga, whether it is a Poorvangavadi or Uththarangavadi raga, and also the approximate time when the raga has to be sung. Vadi note is compared to a king.

🤝 SamVadi

Besides Vadi, Samvadi is more important than other notes. Vadi and Samvadi are co-related to each other. Samvadi is the fifth or fourth note from Vadi (e.g., Yaman: Ga–Ni; Bhoopali: Ga–Da). Samvadi is compared to a minister.

👥 Anuvadi

Apart from Vadi and Samvadi, the remaining notes are called Anuvadi (e.g., in Raga Yaman, Ga is Vadi, Ni is Samvadi, Anuvadi notes are Ri, Ma, Pa, Da). Anuvadi notes are compared to the servant of state.

🚫 Vivadi

Vivadi does not belong to a raga. It can be used sparingly to beautify the raga, but it should be done carefully at the proper place. Otherwise, the whole raga will be spoiled.

🌀 Vakra Swara

While ascending or descending to a desired note, we sometimes make a turn from a note in the middle to the next lower note, then again reach the desired note without stopping anywhere. That turning note is called Vakra Swara (crooked note).
Example: Pa Dha Ni Dha Sa (Ascend) — Ni is crooked note.
Example: Ga Ri Ma Ga (Descend) — Ri is crooked note.

🎵 Alankara (Palta)

The peculiar pattern of a group of notes is known as Alankara. It operates both in ascent and descent (e.g., Sa Ga, Ri Ma, Ga Pa, Ma Dha, Pa Ni, Dha Sa / Sa Dha, Ni Pa, Dha Ma, Pa Ga, Ma Ri, Ga Sa). Alankara helps achieve clear expression and intonation of every note. No one can be a proficient artist without practicing Alankaras.

🗂️ Thata (Mela)

A collection of notes which can produce a raga is called Thata. Rules regarding Thata:

  1. Thata must have seven notes.
  2. Notes must be in serial order: Sa Ri Ga Ma Pa Dha Ni.
  3. No matter to have 2 notes of Komala and Teevra belonging to the same family one after another (Ma Ma).
  4. No need to have both Ascent and Descent; Ascent alone can indicate Thata (e.g., Kalyana: Sa Ri Ga Ma Pa Dha Ni Sa).
  5. No need to have Ranjakata (Music value).

🎶 Raga

A composition of notes which can please the ear and mind is called Raga. Rules regarding Raga:

  1. Raga should be derived from Thata.
  2. Raga should have at least five notes.
  3. Raga should have regular Ascent and Descent.
  4. Ma and Pa should not be omitted simultaneously.
  5. Sa should not be omitted in any circumstance.
  6. Raga should have its own Vadi note.
  7. Raga should have Ranjakata (Music value).
  8. Notes of Komala and Teevra belonging to the same family should not be used one after another (yet a few exceptions are seen: Lalith, Kedar, Behag).

📌 Pakad

Mukhayanga is another name for Pakad. It is the predominant passage of notes of a raga in short — "catch notes" which indicate the peculiar raga. Example: SS RR GG MM P — on hearing this combination, we recognize Raga Kafi.

🎻 Meend

A graceful transition from one note to another without silencing the voice is called Meend. It is of great aesthetic value. In violin, it is played by sliding from one note to another. In sitar, it is played pulling the main wire. Meends are played both upwardly and downwardly: GMP (upward), PMG (downward).

📯 Ghaseet

Ghaseet is a graceful transition from one note to another by a glide without breaking the continuity of sound. Ghaseet is played on sitar. The same action on Sarod, Violin, and Sarangi is called Soot.

🎻 Soot

In Violin, when two or three notes are played with a glide over the notes smoothly and gracefully without breaking the continuity of sound with one action of the bow, it is called Soot. The same action on sitar is called Ghaseet.

💎 Kan

Kan is a grace note. Sometimes a peculiar note is played touching another note slightly. The preceding touched note is the Kan. It is written over the peculiar note. Example: pm

⚡ Toda

Todas are slow and rapid successions of notes intonated in different forms or styles meant for developing Raga. Tans and Todas are one and the same thing. When a Tana is played on sitar, it is called Toda. When the same Tana is sung, it is called Tana.

🎤 Alap

Creates an atmosphere regarding the raga which is going to be sung. As an athlete does some free exercises before the event, the artist performs some phrases of notes in a rhythmic manner, much slower than the tempo of the raga. It is the rhapsodical embellishment of a raga. Alap is used at the beginning, at interval, and at the end.

🎶 Tan

Singing or playing the same lines of raga repeatedly may create monotony. Tans help to give fresh sweetness throughout the raga. In short, tans are open-voiced running passages of notes in quick tempo. Its main aim is to develop the raga with embellishment. Unlike other styles, tans are not used in Dhrupad and Dhamar.

⏱️ Laya

Laya means speed or tempo of a beat (Tala). Laya is mainly three kinds:

  1. Vilambit Laya (slow tempo)
  2. Madhya Laya (medium tempo)
  3. Drut Laya (fast tempo)

Music is controlled by Laya. Vilambit Khyal (Bada Khyal) applies Vilambit Laya, while Drut Khyal (Chota Khyal) applies Madhya and Drut Laya respectively.

🥁 Tal

Tal means beating. Talas are formed by clapping hands or beating on Tala instruments such as Tabla, Mridanga, etc. Tala helps the singer or instrumentalist to perform rhythmically and attentively. Notation of a song cannot be written without the help of Tala.

📜 Theka

The composition of syllables used to play Tala instruments is called Theka. Each Tala has its own number of Matras. Some syllables of tabla are: Dha, Ti, Na, Ga, Thiri, Kita, etc.

🎯 Sam

(Samagraha) It is the principal beat of Tala. It should be played forcefully and attentively. Tala begins and finishes on the Sam. Mukada, Tukata, Tihai, Mohora, Chakkradar finish on Sam.

🕳️ Khali

It is the weak point of Tala, but it is the most important point which helps to recognize the forthcoming Sam. "O" is the symbol of Khali. The hands are separated when showing Khali. Khali is not found in Karnataka music.

👏 Bhari

The places in Tala on which hand beats are shown are defined as Bhari. It is also called Anutala.

⏲️ Matra

Is the shortest measure (unit). It is equivalent to a single pulse or a twinkle of the eye.

🏛️ Ashraya Raga

Also known as Thata Vachaka Raga or Raga which suggests the name of the Raga. There are 10 Ashraya Ragas: Yaman, Bilawal, Kamaj, Bhairava, Poorvi, Marwa, Kafi, Asawari, Bhairavi, and Thodi. Ragas produced from the same Thata have a tinge of the Ashraya Raga.

🎵 Sargam

Sargam Gayan is the rhythmical representation of notes in the shape of songs and the Ragas, which are properly controlled by proper time and rhythm.

🎵 Shuddha Swara

The notes that stand on their originally fixed pitches or natural places in the scale are called Shuddha Swaras (Prakrit Swara): S R G M P D N.

🎵 Vikrit Swara

When the Shuddha Swaras change their original pitches or are lowered or raised, they become Vikrit. Ri, Ga, Dha, Ni (Komal Vikrit) and Ma (Teevra Vikrit).

⬇️ Komal Vikrit

When the sound of Ri, Ga, Dha, Ni (Shuddha) is lowered from their original pitches, they are called Komal Vikrit. Ri Ga Dha Ni

⬆️ Teevra Vikrit

When the sound of Ma is raised from its original pitch, it is called Teevra Vikrit. Ma

🎤 Forms of Singing

🎵 Dhrupad

Dhrupad is the best type of Gayaki. In the 13th century A.D., in the time of Sarangadeva, the Jati Gayan was more popular, and after Jati Gayan, the Dhrupad style of singing came into practice. This type of composition and style of singing originated from Raja Man Singh of Gwalior. He is considered the founder of Dhrupad style and a most proficient composer.

It is sung in slow rhythm. It contains four parts: Sthai (First), Antara (Second), Sanchari (Third), and Abhoga (Fourth). The language of Dhrupad is high and the thoughts are deep. Tan-Paltas are prohibited. This Gayan needs a forceful voice and throat, hence it is mainly sung by male musicians. Veer, Shanti, and Shringar Rasas are prominent. The central theme contains ideas of self-reliance and devotion to god, sung mainly in Chartala and oblique Talas.

🎵 Dhamar

The songs sung in Dhamar Tala are called Dhamar. It is a kind of Hori. A Dhamar depicts the life activities of Sri Krishna, like Rasa Leelas, which are sung to display the life activities of Radha and Krishna in the month of Falgun of Vikram Era. Shringar Rasa is more prominent. Contains four parts: Sthai, Antara, Sanchari, and Abhoga like Dhrupad. These Gayans are sung in Gamak, Meend, Bol Talas, and in Duggun and Chaugun. It also requires a forceful sound.

🎵 Khayal

Khayal is derived from Urdu, meaning "thoughts". This Gayan is very popular nowadays. Anibaddha Gayan was sung as prolongation without time and rhythm. Khyal Gayan came into practice by combining Anibaddha Gayan with Nibaddha Gayan. Amir Khusro tried to popularize this Gayan but it could not compete with Dhrupad. In the period of Mohammad Shah, Adarand and Sadarang took interest in popularizing Khyal Gayan.

Khyal Gayan is of two types:

  • Bada Khayal (slow khayal) — sung in Vilambit Laya, Teental, Ektal, and Ada Chartal.
  • Chhota Khayal (fast khayal) — sung in Teentala and Jhaptala.

Shringar Rasa plays the prominent role. How the musician expresses his art by prolongation depends upon individual effort. Poetic words are not given importance compared to prolongation.

🎵 Thumari

Thumari Gayan started from Nawab families about 200 years ago. It is sweet and popular like Khayal, but the musician is not expected to maintain the correctness and purity of Raga like Khayal. Beauty of Gayaki expresses the prominent part of the song. Thumari is sung in Kafi, Bhairavi, and Khamaj Thatas, in Teen Tala, Kaharwa, and Dadra. Thumaries mainly depict the life activities of Radha and Krishna. They are more popular in Brijmandal and in eastern U.P.

🎵 Tarana

This Gayan is sung through words like Tom, Tana, Ta, Dir, Da, and Nee instead of songs. This Gayan is rhythmical and correctness of Raga is the chief characteristic. Musicians nowadays sing this for recreation only. Tarana was started in the times of Allauddin Khilji, who originated it to impart musical education to foreigners who were not familiar with Indian musical symbols. He taught them Ragas through the above bols.

🎵 Lakshan Geet

The Gayan which tells us the special features of Raga is called Lakshan Geet. It makes us known with the name and theory of Raga, its ascent and descent, and time of singing, etc. The Lakshan Geet is sung in time and rhythm of the Raga to which it belongs. It clearly explains the special features of its mother Raga.

🎵 Sargam

Sargam Gayan is the rhythmical representation of notes in the shape of songs and the Ragas which are properly controlled by proper time and rhythm.

📜 Dhatu

Dhatu means the different parts of Prabandha, Vastu, Roopak, etc. In ancient times, Prabandha, Vastu, Roopak had different parts called Udgraha, Melapak, Dhruva, Antara, and Abhog, which were called "Dhatu".

👤 Great Personalities in Indian Music

🌟 Pandith Vishnunrayan Bhatkhande

He is honored as the founder of modern Hindustani music. Born in Bombay on August 13, 1860, he took to flute playing at the early age of ten. He studied sitar and later passed his law final examination, practicing as a lawyer for some time. After his wife and only child died, he turned to music to banish loneliness. Having studied Dhrupad and Khayal vocal music from experts like Raojibuwa Belbagkkar, Ali Hussain, and Vilayathkhan, he decided to standardize North Indian music which was in disarray.

He traveled all over India meeting renowned musicians and collecting their compositions, and studied famous music books. He also studied South Indian music and understood 72 Melakartas. He selected 10 Melakartas and constructed the ten Thats of North Indian music. For the first time, he founded a notation system and published six volumes titled "Kramik Pustak Malika". He wrote many useful books: "Rag Trangani", "Rag Manjhari", "Raga Tatwa Vibhoda", "Sangeeta Shastra", etc.

In 1916, he convened the first music conference in Baroda. He convened the second in 1919 and the third in 1929. He established the "Marris College of Music" in 1929 in Lucknow, later known as the Bhatkhande College of Hindustani Music at Kaiderbagh, Lucknow. This became the first music college in North India. Students from all over the world come here to study Ragadhari music, Vocal, Instrument, and Dance. North Indian classical music reached Sri Lanka through this esteemed institution. He is called the father of modern North Indian classical music. He left this world on September 14, 1936.

🌟 Dr. S.N. Rathanjankar

He was the disciple of late Pandit Vishnunarayan Bhatkhande, born in Bombay on December 31, 1900. He started music lessons at age seven under Pandit Krishna Bhat. Pandit Bhatkhande took him under his studentship in 1911 and sent him to the great musician of Agra Gharana, Ustad Faizkhan, for further study. He was later appointed principal of the Bhatkhande College of Music, Lucknow. He was elected Vice Chancellor of the "Indira Kala Sangit" Viswavidyalaya of Khairagarh M.P. In 1957, he was awarded the honorary title of Padma Bhushan.

He published informative articles on classical music and composed about 200 rare compositions under "Geeta Manjari" (two volumes). He also wrote "Tan Sangraha" in three volumes, helping students comprehend "Tanalamkaras". He composed new Ragas like Marga Bihag, Kedar Bahar, Savni Kedar, Salagavarai, Rajani Kalyan, and published "Varnams" and "Kritis" of Carnatic music in Sanskrit for music lovers.

🌟 Amir Khusro

Amir Khusro was the first great Muslim musicologist of India. Though born in the East district of North India, his grandparents were from Turkey, so he called himself a Hindu Turk. He was a disciple of Hazrat Nazamuddin Aulia and became a philosopher, scholar, poet, and musician. He was a dignified employee at the imperial court of Delhi under successive kings including Allauddin Khilji.

He fused Persian-Arabic elements with Indian music and made it elegant. His name is recorded as the inventor of the popular instrument "Sitar" (from Persian "She-Tar" meaning three-stringed). It is believed that the pair of Tabla is also an invention of Amir Khusro — he cut the two-faced Pakhavaj into two pieces to make Tabla.

He created new Ragas: "Sarjarda" and "Zilaph". He created the famous religious singing style "Qawwali" and was the originator of the important singing style "Tarana".

🎻 Violin — Tuning & Buying Guide

🔧 How to Tune a Violin

A violin is one of the hardest instruments to tune. You must have a trained ear for music and pitch or you will not be able to tune a violin on your own. Tightening the strings too much will cause the strings to wear or even break. If the violin is not perfectly tuned, the music will never sound quite right.

Things You'll Need: Violin · Tuner or piano · Bow · Rosin

  1. Purchase a tuner if you do not have one. Find one that makes sounds for all four strings: E, A, D, and G. You can use a piano or keyboard, but they may be slightly off, especially if not tuned properly.
  2. Rosin the bow to make sure the bow brings out a good clear tone. If not done properly, it will make tuning much harder.
  3. Tune the "A" string first. Play the note on the tuner or piano. Match the sound with your violin. Use the pegs to get as close as possible, then use the fine tuner to get it exact.
  4. Repeat for the other three strings or, if you have a trained ear, use the harmony of fifths to tune perfectly. Tune a string by playing it at the same time as the "A" string. Adjust until the vibrations disappear and the strings are in perfect harmony.

🛒 How to Buy a Violin — Tips for Beginners

  • Rental vs Purchase: Rentals are generally lesser-quality instruments. If you rent for more than a year, you may have already paid through the value of the instrument. Some shops let you apply part of rental fees towards purchase.
  • For children: Consider rental or a "trade-in policy" with a reputable luthier or music store. Most places will not give a trade-in discount for an instrument you did not buy from them.
  • If buying full-size: Go to a violin dealer or luthier (a person who makes or repairs stringed instruments). Bring an experienced violinist or luthier with you.
  • Test the instrument: It should be solid to the touch with no creaks. All open strings should sound full, resonant, and pleasing to the ear.
  • Buying higher-quality: Violin, bow, and case are often sold separately. Test them before buying. Negotiate the price. Try instruments in different rooms (large halls to practice rooms) to assess capabilities.
  • Bow considerations: Needs good weight, flexibility, and balance between frog and tip. Too heavy can strain your hand; too light makes it difficult to produce a big sound. If too flexible or too stiff, it will be less nimble.
  • Modern vs older instruments: Older instruments are valuable for sound, beauty, and antique value. Modern makers' instruments sound every bit as good and are more budget-friendly.
  • Expectations of value: A violin is an investment, but don't expect dramatic appreciation on a violin purchased for less than $100,000. Most violins hold their value if you trade for a more expensive violin from the same dealer.

📐 Student, Intermediate & Pro Levels

  • Student Level: Produced for beginners, often machine-made. Maple used for high friction parts (pegs, fingerboard), dyed to resemble Ebony. Excellent for early stages, priced to fit budgets.
  • Intermediate Level: Better quality wood and workmanship, mostly hand-made. Pegs and fingerboard usually made of Ebony. Extensive hand graduation gives a more refined sound. Some may approach professional level.
  • Pro Level: Made from finest woods with fanatical attention to detail. Few craftsmen skilled at this level, so prices are considerably higher.

🔍 Parts of a Violin

  • Body: The "box" part. The top is made of thinly shaved spruce; the back and sides (ribs) are made of maple.
  • Neck Assembly: Structure attached to the top end. Made of maple, with peg box, scroll, fingerboard, and nut.
  • Bridge: Specially shaped piece of hard maple that sits between strings and body, transmitting vibrations.
  • Sound Post: Small cylindrical piece wedged between back and face. Placement affects sound greatly.
  • F-Hole: Two holes cut in the top to project sound from the interior.
  • Button: Small round piece fitted by pressure into a hole in the bottom ribs. Anchoring point for the tailgut.
  • Tailpiece: Long tapered piece suspended above the top by the ends of the strings at the bridge end.
  • Tailgut: Long strand attaching through holes in the bottom end of the tailpiece, looping around the button.
  • String Adjuster (optional): Small mechanical device attached to the tailpiece for fine adjustments.
