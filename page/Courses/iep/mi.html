🎵 Pt. Vyankatmakhi and his 72 Thatas 72 Melakartas

Dahanake Music Academy
Sri Lanka's No. 1 Music School

In the history of music in the South of India the name of Pt. Vyankatmakhi is brilliant and predominant. He was the court musician of Tanjore Raja. Govinda Dixit was his father, who carried Guru Parampara of his teacher Tanappacharya. He wrote his famous treatise on music, Chaturdandi Prakashika (1640 AD). He is a lyricist too; his songs are still sung throughout South India. He was the pioneer of 72 Melakartas.

🔢 Construction of 72 Melakartas

This is how musicologist Vyankatmakhi constructed 72 Melakartas mathematically. Write 12 Shuddha and Vikrit Swaras in serial order as shown below:

S R R G G M Ṃ P D D N N

Now keep aside Teevra Ṃ for a moment and add Tar S at the end, in the following manner:

S R R G G M P D D N N S

Then divide them into 2 equal parts:

S R R G G M / P D D N N S

Now we can construct 6 different varieties of half scale of 4 notes each part — which means 12 half scales of 4 notes in total according to notes introduced as in the South.

#Half Scale (First Part)Half Scale (Second Part)
1S R R MP D D S
2S R G MP D N S
3S R G MP D N S
4S R G MP D N S
5S R G MP D N S
6S G G MP N N S

Now join all the six half scales of the first part with each of the six half scales of the second part. By this method of permutation of notes and combination of half scales we can obtain 36 melakartas mathematically in total.

Besides, by using Theevra Ṃ, which was omitted before, we can get additional 36 melakartas. So the total number of melakartas become 72 (with Komal M = 36, with Teevra Ṃ = 36). Yet only 19 melakartas are selected for the classification of Ragas in Karnataki music.

🎶 Modern Theory of Ten Thata — Pt. V. N. Bhatkhande

Pt. V. N. Bhatkhande constructed only 32 Thatas omitting the first line and the sixth line of the illustration (1st scale and sixth scale) according to Hindustani music.

16 Thatas can be constructed by using Komal M, and another 16 Thatas can be constructed by using Teevra Ṃ. Hence Pt. Bhatkhande calculated the number of Thatas as 32. Yet only 10 Thatas are selected for the classification of Raga, viz.

📊 Origin of Ragas and their Number

It is understood that 72 Thatas or Melakartas can be obtained from 12 notes (Shuddha & Vikrit) of the scale. By means of nine Jatis we can construct 484 Ragas from Bilawal Thata alone. Moreover 34,848 Ragas can be constructed mathematically from all the 72 Thatas.

Each Raga has its own number of notes in the Ascent and in the Descent. It is called Jati of a Raga. There are mainly 3 groups of Jati:

  • Sampurna – Sampurna — 7 notes both in Ascent and Descent
  • Shadava – Shadava — 6 notes both in Ascent and Descent
  • Oudava – Oudava — 5 notes both in Ascent and Descent

In addition they are again classified into 6 groups. The total number of Jati becomes nine.

📖 Short Descriptions — Key Terms

Graha SwaraStarting note of a Raga. In ancient times there was a prescribed note for the beginning. Today singers start on any note extempore.
Nyas SwaraPrescribed note for the conclusion of a Raga. Modern concept: getting stoppage on any note while rendering an Alapa — can be used on Vadi, Samvadi or any important note.
Ansha SwaraPredominant and life-note of a Raga. Frequently used.
Poorva RagaRagas sung between 12 noon and 12 midnight.
Uttara RagaRagas sung between 12 midnight and 12 noon next day.
Poorvanga-Vadi RagaVadi note in the first part of the octave (S R G M or S R G M P). Sung from 12 noon to 12 midnight.
Uttaranga-Vadi RagaVadi note in the second part of the octave (P D N S or M P D N S). Sung from 12 midnight to 12 noon.
Sandhi Prakash RagaRagas sung at the junction of day and night (sunrise 4–7 AM, sunset 4–7 PM). Carry "R" and "Dha" Komal. Derived from Bhairava, Poorvi and Marwa Thatas.
Shuddha RagaRagas appearing in pure form with their own resources independently. e.g. Yaman, Bhairava, Todi.
Chayalaga RagaRagas with shadow from another Raga. Made up of two different Ragas. e.g. Gauda-Mallar, Ahir-Bhairava.
Sankeerna RagaRagas performed by the mixture of more than two Ragas. e.g. Peelu (Bhairavi + Bhimpalasi + Gauri).

🎯 Raga and Thata — Rules & Definitions

🗂️ Thata (Mela)

A collection of notes which can produce a Raga is called Thata. Rules:

  1. Thata must have seven notes.
  2. Notes must be in serial order: S R G M P D N.
  3. No matter to have 2 notes of Komal and Teevra belonging to the same family one after another (e.g. M Ṃ).
  4. No need to have both Ascent and Descent — Ascent alone can indicate Thata (e.g. Kalyan: S R G Ṃ P D N S).
  5. No need to have Ranjakata (music value).

🎶 Raga

A composition of notes which can please the ear and mind is called Raga. Rules:

  1. Raga should be derived from a Thata.
  2. Raga should have at least five notes.
  3. Raga should have regular Ascent and Descent.
  4. Ma and Pa should not be omitted simultaneously.
  5. Sa should not be omitted in any circumstance.
  6. Raga should have its own Vadi note.
  7. Raga should have Ranjakata (music value).
  8. Notes of Komal and Teevra belonging to the same family should not be used one after another (yet few exceptions: Lalith, Kedar, Behag).

🏛️ Ashraya Raga

Also known as Thata Vachaka Raga — Raga which suggests the name of the Thata. There are 10 Ashraya Ragas: Yaman, Bilawal, Kamaj, Bhairava, Poorvi, Marwa, Kafi, Asawari, Bhairavi, and Todi.

⭐ Importance of Vadi Swara

  • The most important note of a Raga is called Vadi Swara. It is predominant and most frequently used. It is regarded as a king.
  • Each Raga has its own Vadi Swara — the life note (Ansha Swara).
  • Vadi Swara can be recognized by its frequent use, longest stoppage, starting and finishing on it.
  • Vadi decides the Time of Singing — indicates whether a Raga is Poorvanga or Uttaranga.
  • A collection of notes attached to Vadi incites a Raga.
  • Some other Raga can be formed by changing the Vadi note.
  • As a rule, the development of a Raga is performed from the Vadi note.
  • The entire embellishment depends on Vadi Swara.

Example: In Raga Yaman, G is Vadi (Poorvanga) → sung in first quarter of night.
In Raga Bilawal, D is Vadi (Uttaranga) → sung in first quarter of day.

🎭 Rag-Ragini Paddhati

Till the 13th century AD there was only one system of music prevalent all over India — Bharatiya Sangeeth Paddhati (Grama-Moorchana-Jati system). After Sharangadeva (13th century), Narada (author of Sangeeth Makaranda) introduced the Raga-Ragini system in the North.

Rag-Ragini is classified into: Raga, Ragini, Putra Raga, Putra Badhu.

There were different schools regarding Rag-Ragini system:

  • Someshwar School — Principal Ragas: Shri, Vasant, Pancham, Bhairava, Megh, Nat-Narayana
  • Bharat School — Principal Ragas: Bhairava, Malkaus, Hindol, Deepak, Megh, Shri
  • Kallinath School — Similar to Someshwar but different Raginis
  • Hanuman School — Similar to Bharat but different Raginis
  • Indra-Prastha School — Principal Ragas: Bhairava, Kaishik, Hindol, Deepak, Sri, Megh

In 1813, Mohammed Raza of Patna exploded a new Rag-Ragini system. He accepted Bhairava, Malkans, Hindol, Shri, Megh and Nat as principal Ragas and produced 36 Raginis. He also accepted Bilawal as the Shuddha (natural) scale.

Rag-Ragini system is no longer current today. Pt. V. N. Bhatkhande boldly declared all opinions of Rag-Ragini were imaginative and unscientific, and the That-Raga system was correct and scientific.

⏰ Time Theory of Ragas (Time-Circle)

There is a specific period for performing Ragas — a distinguishing feature of Hindustani music. All Ragas are classified under 3 groups:

  1. Ragas having both R and D Teevra — sung after R, D Komal Ragas. Belong to Yaman, Bilawal, Kamaj scales. Sung 7–10 AM and 7–10 PM.
  2. Ragas having both R and D Komal — Sandhi-Prakash Ragas (twilight). Belong to Bhairava, Poorvi, Marwa melas. Sung 4–7 AM and 4–7 PM.
  3. Ragas having both G and N Komal — belong to Kafi, Asawari, Bhairavi, Todi Scales. Sung 10 AM–4 PM and 10 PM–4 AM.

Key Rule: Most Ragas having Komal M are sung in the day, while Ragas having Teevra Ṃ are sung in the night. (Few exceptions: Sohini, Ramkali, Hindol, Todi, Multani — sung in the day despite having Teevra Ṃ.)

The position of the Vadi note decides the time of singing:

  • Poorva Ragas — Vadi in first part (SRGM or SRGMP) → sung 12 noon to 12 midnight.
  • Uttar Ragas — Vadi in second part (PDNS or MPDNS) → sung 12 midnight to 12 noon.

📖 Musical Terms — Extended Definitions

VadiPredominant and life note of the raga. Most frequently used; long stoppages done on it. Compared to a king.
SamVadiCo-related to Vadi; fifth or fourth note from Vadi. Compared to a minister. e.g. Yaman: Ga–Ni, Bhoopali: Ga–Da.
AnuvadiRemaining notes apart from Vadi and Samvadi. Compared to servants of state.
VivadiDoes not belong to a raga. Used sparingly to beautify. Must be used carefully.
Vakra SwaraA "crooked" note — a turn from a note in the middle to the next lower note, then reaching the desired note without stopping. e.g. Pa Dha Ni Dha Sa (Ni is crooked).
Alankara (Palta)Peculiar pattern of a group of notes. Operates in both ascent and descent. Helps achieve clear expression and intonation.
PakadMukhayanga — predominant passage of notes in short. "Catch notes" that indicate the peculiar raga. e.g. SS RR ḠḠ MM P → Raga Kafi.
MeendGraceful transition from one note to another without silencing the voice. In violin, sliding; in sitar, pulling the main wire.
GhaseetGraceful transition from one note to another by a glide without breaking continuity. Played on sitar. Same action on Sarod, Violin, Sarangi = Soot.
SootIn Violin, when two or three notes are played with a glide smoothly and gracefully with one action of the bow. Same action on sitar = Ghaseet.
KanA grace note. A peculiar note touched slightly by another note. The preceding touched note is the Kan. e.g. pm (M is the Kan).
TodaSlow and rapid successions of notes in different forms meant for developing Raga. Tans and Todas are the same; when played on sitar = Toda, when sung = Tana.
AlapCreates an atmosphere regarding the raga. Rhapsodical embellishment. Used at the beginning, interval, and end.
TanOpen-voiced running passages of notes in quick tempo. Aims to develop the raga with embellishment. Not used in Dhrupad and Dhamar.
LayaSpeed or tempo of a beat. Three kinds: Vilambit (slow), Madhya (medium), Drut (fast).
TalMeans beating. Formed by clapping hands or beating on Tala instruments (Tabla, Mridanga). Helps perform rhythmically.
ThekaComposition of syllables used to play Tala instruments. Each Tala has its own number of Matras. e.g. Dha, Ti, Na, Ga, Thiri, Kita.
Sam(Samagraha) Principal beat of Tala. Tala begins and finishes on Sam. Mukada, Tukata, Tihai, Mohora, Chakkradar finish on Sam.
KhaliWeak point of Tala, but most important — helps recognize the forthcoming Sam. Symbol "O". Hands separated when showing Khali. Not found in Karnataka music.
BhariPlaces in Tala on which hand beats are shown. Also called Anutala.
MatraShortest measure (unit) — equivalent to a single pulse or a twinkle of the eye.
SargamRhythmical representation of notes in the shape of songs and Ragas, properly controlled by time and rhythm.
Shuddha SwaraNotes that stand on their originally fixed pitches: S R G M P D N (Prakrit Swara).
Vikrit SwaraWhen Shuddha Swaras change their original pitches — lowered or raised. Ṛ Ḡ Ḍ Ṇ (Komal Vikrit) and Ṃ (Teevra Vikrit).
Komal VikritWhen sound of Ṛ Ḡ Ḍ Ṇ is lowered from original pitches.
Teevra VikritWhen sound of Ma is raised from its original pitch: Ṃ.

🎤 Forms of Singing

🎵 Dhrupad

The best type of Gayaki. Originated from Raja Man Singh of Gwalior. Sung in slow rhythm. Contains four parts: Sthai, Antara, Sanchari, and Abhoga. Tan-Paltas are prohibited. Needs a forceful voice. Veer, Shanti, and Shringar Rasas are prominent.

🎵 Dhamar

Songs sung in Dhamar Tala. Depicts life activities of Sri Krishna (Rasa Leelas). Shringar Rasa is more prominent. Contains four parts like Dhrupad. Sung in Gamak, Meend, Bol Talas, and in Duggun and Chaugun.

🎵 Khayal

Derived from Urdu meaning "thoughts". Very popular today. Amir Khusro tried to popularize it; later Adarand and Sadarang took interest. Two types:

  • Bada Khayal — slow (Vilambit Laya) — Teental, Ektal, Ada Chartal
  • Chhota Khayal — fast (Drut Laya) — Teentala, Jhaptala

🎵 Thumari

Started from Nawab families about 200 years ago. Sweet and popular. Musician not expected to maintain purity of Raga like Khayal. Beauty of Gayaki is prominent. Sung in Kafi, Bhairavi, Khamaj Thatas, in Teen Tala, Kaharwa, Dadara.

🎵 Tarana

Sung through words like Tom, Tana, Ta, Dir, Da, Nee instead of songs. Rhythmical; correctness of Raga is chief characteristic. Started by Amir Khusro to impart musical education to foreigners.

🎵 Lakshan Geet

Tells the special features of Raga — name, theory, ascent, descent, time of singing, etc. Sung in time and rhythm of the Raga to which it belongs.

🎵 Sargam

Rhythmical representation of notes in the shape of songs and Ragas, properly controlled by time and rhythm.

📜 Dhatu

Different parts of Prabandha, Vastu, Roopak — Udgraha, Melapak, Dhruva, Antara, Abhog.

🎻 Violin — Tuning & Buying Guide

🔧 How to Tune a Violin

A violin is one of the hardest instruments to tune. You must have a trained ear for music and pitch. Tightening the strings too much will cause wear or even break.

Things You'll Need: Violin · Tuner or piano · Bow · Rosin

  1. Purchase a tuner that makes sounds for all four strings: E, A, D, G.
  2. Rosin the bow to bring out a good clear tone.
  3. Tune the "A" string first. Match the sound. Use pegs to get close, then fine tuner to get exact.
  4. Repeat for the other three strings or use harmony of fifths to tune perfectly.

🛒 How to Buy a Violin — Tips

  • Rental vs Purchase: Rentals are generally lesser-quality. If you rent for more than a year, you may have already paid through the value. Some shops apply part of rental fees towards purchase.
  • For children: Consider rental or a "trade-in policy" with a reputable luthier or music store.
  • If buying full-size: Go to a violin dealer or luthier. Bring an experienced violinist or luthier with you.
  • Test the instrument: Solid to the touch with no creaks. All open strings should sound full, resonant, pleasing.
  • Bow considerations: Needs good weight, flexibility, balance between frog and tip.
  • Modern vs older instruments: Older instruments are valuable for sound, beauty, antique value. Modern makers' instruments sound every bit as good and are more budget-friendly.

📐 Student, Intermediate & Pro Levels

  • Student Level: Machine-made, maple used for high friction parts, dyed to resemble Ebony. Excellent for early stages.
  • Intermediate Level: Better wood and workmanship, mostly hand-made. Ebony pegs and fingerboard. More refined sound.
  • Pro Level: Finest woods, fanatical attention to detail. Few craftsmen skilled at this level; prices considerably higher.

🔍 Parts of a Violin

  • Body: Top made of spruce, back and sides (ribs) of maple.
  • Neck Assembly: Maple with peg box, scroll, fingerboard, nut.
  • Bridge: Hard maple piece that sits between strings and body, transmitting vibrations.
  • Sound Post: Small cylindrical piece wedged between back and face. Placement affects sound greatly.
  • F-Hole: Two holes cut in the top to project sound from the interior.
  • Button: Small round piece fitted into a hole in the bottom ribs. Anchoring point for the tailgut.
  • Tailpiece: Long tapered piece suspended above the top by the ends of the strings.
  • Tailgut: Long strand attaching through holes in the bottom end of the tailpiece, looping around the button.
  • String Adjuster (optional): Small mechanical device for fine adjustments.
